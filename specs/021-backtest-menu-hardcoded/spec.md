## Clarifications

### Session 2026-07-24 (wide-range structural-stop variant)

New variant added: "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (wide-range structural stop)" (User Story 1d, FR-032–FR-035). It reuses the base strategy and changes exactly two things — a 9h-range entry filter and the stop-loss mechanism.

- Q: What is the 9h-range filter? → A: The day is only traded when the H1 reference range (H1 high − H1 low, FR-003) is **strictly greater than 40 points**. A day whose H1 range is 40 points or less is a no-trade day (no candidate search, no trades) — reported as "no trade" (FR-012), not "no data" (the H1 candle exists).
- Q: What replaces the fixed 50-point stop-loss? → A: A **structural stop**. While break-even is unarmed, the position is stopped when a 5-minute candle **closes** beyond the H1 reference level on the losing side — for a long, a candle closing below the H1 low; for a short, closing above the H1 high — filled at that candle's close (a market exit, so the FR-010 gap-fill convention does not apply, mirroring the time-cut). The tunable stop-loss distance (FR-025, default 50) is unused by this variant.
- Q: Does break-even (FR-008a) still apply? → A: **Yes**, unchanged. It arms at +20 points favorable and moves the stop to the entry price. Once armed, that entry-price (break-even) stop is the active stop and supersedes the structural stop — consistent with break-even only ever moving the stop upward. The structural stop therefore applies only while break-even is unarmed.
- Q: Does take-profit (H1 high − 10 for a long / H1 low + 10 for a short, FR-008) still apply? → A: **Yes**, unchanged. Only the stop mechanism and the range filter differ from the base.
- Q: Exit ordering when a candle both reaches take-profit and closes beyond the H1 level? → A: While unarmed, the structural stop is a **close** event (the candle's last tick) whereas take-profit is reached intrabar (earlier), so **take-profit wins**. Once break-even is armed the stop is a price level and the base FR-009 ordering applies (break-even before take-profit).
- Q: Are the four tunable thresholds (FR-025) still in effect? → A: Take-profit offset (10), break-even trigger (20) and max entry distance (20) apply identically; the stop-loss distance is unused. The 40-point range threshold is a fixed property of this variant, not added to Backtest Parameters (like the time-cut's 30/5).
- Q: New backtest or a change to the existing ones? → A: A **third** hardcoded backtest, selectable from the same menu, so its results can be compared against the original and the time-cut variant. The first two backtests are unchanged.

### Session 2026-07-24 (candle caching)

- Q: What gets cached — the computed backtest result (trades, exit reasons, points) or the raw Saxo candle data? → A: Raw candle data only — the 9:00–10:00 H1 reference candle and the 5-minute candles from 10:00 to end of day for a given trading day. The strategy evaluation (breakout/reversal detection, exits, thresholds) still runs fresh against those candles on every request; nothing about the computed `Trade`/`DayResult`/summary output is ever cached. This means a future change to the strategy logic or to the tunable thresholds (FR-025) is reflected immediately, with no cache invalidation needed.
- Q: What is the cache key? → A: The pair (backtest definition code, trading date) — e.g. `B9H` / 2026-07-14. Each hardcoded backtest gets its own cached copy of that day's FRA40.I candles; "CAC40 Bougie de 9h", "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)", and "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (wide-range structural stop)" each cache the same underlying candles separately under their own definition codes. This duplicates identical candle data across the three backtests, which is accepted for simplicity — the cache is a capability of the Backtest feature, not a generic candle cache shared with other parts of the system.
- Q: Do cached entries expire? → A: No — no TTL, permanent cache. A backtest only ever evaluates already-closed historical trading days (Assumptions), so a cached day's candles do not change once stored. If a cached day is ever found to hold incorrect Saxo data, correcting it is a manual data-maintenance action (clearing that entry directly), not an in-product refresh flow.
- Q: Is a day with no H1 reference candle (holiday, missing data) also cached? → A: Yes — caching the "no data" outcome for that (definition, date) pair avoids repeatedly calling Saxo for a day already known to have no usable reference candle.
- Q: Does this apply to both the single-day and range runs? → A: Yes — a range run evaluates each day in the range the same way a single-day run does, so both paths share the same per-day cache; running the same day again, alone or as part of any range, is a cache hit as long as the backtest definition matches.

### Session 2026-07-22 (time-cut variant)

- Q: Is the time-cut rule a new mode/parameter on the existing backtest, or a separate hardcoded backtest? → A: A separate hardcoded backtest ("CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)"), selectable alongside the original, so a trader can compare both. The original "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" is unchanged.
- Q: From when is the 30 minutes measured, and against what is the 5-point favorable move judged? → A: From entry (the confirming candle's time, FR-007/FR-021). The favorable move is the trade's max favorable excursion since entry — for a long, how far the candle high has risen above the entry price; for a short, how far the candle low has fallen below it.
- Q: "Never been higher than 5 points" — is exactly 5 points enough to keep the position, or is it still cut? → A: Inclusive — a best move of exactly 5 points does not spare the position; only a move strictly greater than 5 points keeps it. The cut fires when the max favorable excursion is ≤ 5 points.
- Q: At what price is the position cut? → A: At market on the first 5-minute candle at or after the 30-minute mark — that candle's close. It is not a price-level exit, so the gap-fill convention (FR-010) does not apply.
- Q: How does the time-cut interact with the existing exits? → A: It is checked after stop-loss/break-even and take-profit on the same candle (those still win if reached), and before the end-of-day fallback. A trade that reaches its stop or target before the 30-minute mark is unaffected; a trade entered within 30 minutes of the close is never time-cut and exits end-of-day.
- Q: Are the 30 minutes and 5 points tunable per run like the four thresholds (FR-025)? → A: No — they are fixed properties of this hardcoded variant, not added to Backtest Parameters. The four existing thresholds still apply to both backtests.

### Session 2026-07-21 (parametrization)

- Q: Which parts of the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest become tunable — the numeric thresholds, or also the instrument, reference window, and candle horizons? → A: Only the four numeric strategy thresholds (stop-loss distance, take-profit offset, break-even trigger, and max entry distance). The instrument (FRA40.I), the 9:00–10:00 reference window, and the 5-minute/H1 candle horizons stay fixed — they define the shape of the setup, not a knob a trader tunes.
- Q: Are the thresholds configured globally (server/config) or supplied per run? → A: Per run — each backtest request may override any subset of them; an omitted threshold falls back to its default. Nothing is persisted; a run's parameters live only for that request.
- Q: What are the defaults, and do they change existing behavior? → A: Defaults equal the previously hardcoded values (stop-loss 50, take-profit offset 10, break-even trigger 20, max entry distance 20). A run that supplies no parameters is identical to the pre-parametrization behavior.
- Q: Does making the thresholds tunable turn this into a generic backtest engine? → A: No. The strategy logic — reference-range derivation, breakout/reversal detection, exit ordering, one-position-at-a-time — remains fixed in code. Only the four magnitudes are inputs. The Backtest menu still offers a fixed set of hardcoded strategies, not a strategy-authoring tool (FR-002).
- Q: Must the thresholds be positive? → A: Yes — each supplied threshold must be strictly greater than 0; a non-positive value is rejected with a validation error before any day is evaluated.

### Session 2026-07-21 (short positions)

- Q: How should the short-position capability be exposed — a direction toggle, a separate backtest, or always both directions? → A: The single "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest always evaluates both long and short together; there is no direction toggle. Long-only is no longer a separate mode.
- Q: When both directions are evaluated, can a long and a short position be open at the same time? → A: No — at most one position (long OR short) is open at any time. Whichever direction confirms a valid breakout first opens the position and holds it until it closes; the search for both directions only resumes once flat again.
- Q: If a single 5-minute candle would confirm both a long and a short entry at once, which wins? → A: The long entry takes precedence (a deterministic tiebreak). In practice this is unreachable, because the price move that breaks the opposite extreme (the H1 high for a short, the H1 low for a long) coincides with the open position's take-profit — see the edge cases.
- Q: Is the initial breach of the H1 range measured on the candle's close or on any intrabar touch (wick)? → A: On the **close** (added after PR review). A breach requires a 5-minute candle that *closes* outside the H1 range — below the H1 low for a long, above the H1 high for a short. A candle that only pierces the range intrabar but closes back inside does not arm a breach. This matches the criterion already used to discard a pending candidate and re-arm a fresh breach (FR-006b/FR-020), making the breach close-based throughout. Wherever the requirements or scenarios below say price "trades below the H1 low" / "trades above the H1 high" for the *breach*, read it as "closes" outside the range; the later breakout *confirmation* (FR-006b/FR-020) is still an intrabar high/low trade past the candidate's extreme.

### Session 2026-07-14

- Q: In what timezone are the 9:00–10:00 H1 reference window and the 10:00 evaluation start point defined? → A: Paris exchange local time (Europe/Paris), DST-aware — 9:00 always means 9:00 at the exchange, year-round.
- Q: Should the UI enforce an explicit maximum date range for a backtest run, or can a trader request any range? → A: No explicit UI cap — any range is accepted; days beyond what Saxo's historical-candle capability can supply fall through the existing "no data" per-day handling (FR-004).
- Q: Are a Backtest Run's results persisted for later retrieval, or computed fresh on each request? → A: Ephemeral for now — computed on demand and returned synchronously; nothing is persisted.
- Q: What should happen when a trader submits an invalid date range (end date before start date, or a future date)? → A: Reject upfront with a validation error before running anything, rather than silently returning an empty/zero result.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Run the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest for a single day (Priority: P1)

A trader wants to select the "Backtest" menu, pick the hardcoded "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest, choose a past trading day, and see whether the strategy would have entered one or more trades that day, and for each, at what price it entered, exited, and with what result in points.

**Why this priority**: This is the core capability of the feature. Without a single-day run producing a correct trade (or correctly reporting no trade), the feature delivers no value.

**Independent Test**: Run the backtest against a known historical day for FRA40.I where the 9:00–10:00 H1 candle and subsequent 5-minute candles are known in advance. Verify the reported outcome (no trade / trade with entry, exit, and points result) matches manual calculation using the rules below.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a trading day where, after 10:00, FRA40.I trades below the 9:00–10:00 H1 candle's low, a later 5-minute candle closes back above that H1 low, and a subsequent 5-minute candle's high then trades above that reversal candle's high (breakout confirmation), **When** the backtest runs for that day, **Then** the system reports a long trade entered at the reversal candle's high (or at the confirming candle's open price if it gapped above that high).
2. **Given** an open trade from Scenario 1, **When** a subsequent 5-minute candle's price action reaches a loss of 50 points from the entry price before any other exit condition is met, **Then** the system reports the trade closed at that stop-loss level (or at the candle's open price if the candle gapped past the stop-loss level).
3. **Given** an open trade from Scenario 1, **When** price rises to reach the H1 candle's high minus 10 points before the stop-loss or end of day is reached, **Then** the system reports the trade closed at that take-profit level (or at the candle's open price if the candle gapped past it).
4. **Given** an open trade from Scenario 1 that hits neither the stop-loss nor the take-profit level, **When** the trading day ends, **Then** the system reports the trade closed at the closing price of the last 5-minute candle of the day, labeled as an end-of-day exit.
5. **Given** a trading day where price never trades below the 9:00–10:00 H1 low after 10:00, or trades below it but no subsequent 5-minute candle closes back above it, or closes back above it but no later 5-minute candle's high ever breaks above that reversal candle's high before the session ends, **When** the backtest runs for that day, **Then** the system reports no trade for that day.
6. **Given** a trading day where no 9:00–10:00 H1 candle data exists for FRA40.I (e.g., market holiday, missing data), **When** the backtest runs for that day, **Then** the system reports that day as having no data, without failing the run.
7. **Given** a first trade from Scenario 1 that has closed (stop-loss, break-even, take-profit, or a later re-entry closing before end of day), **When** price subsequently trades below the H1 low again, a later 5-minute candle closes back above it, and a subsequent candle confirms the breakout, **Then** the system reports a second trade entered on the same day, evaluated against the same H1 high/low reference levels and the same exit rules, independently of the first trade's outcome.
8. **Given** an open trade from Scenario 1, **When** a subsequent 5-minute candle's high reaches 20 points or more above the entry price, **Then** the system moves that trade's stop-loss up to the entry price (break-even) for evaluation of all following candles, and this adjustment happens at most once for that trade.
9. **Given** an open trade whose stop-loss has already been moved to break-even (Scenario 8), **When** a later 5-minute candle's low reaches the entry price or below, before the take-profit or end of day is reached, **Then** the system reports the trade closed at break-even (0 points), labeled as a "break-even" exit — distinct from a stop-loss, take-profit, or end-of-day exit.

### User Story 1b - Take the mirror-image short trade off the H1 high (Priority: P1)

A trader wants the same "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest to also catch the short version of the setup: after 10:00, when FRA40.I trades **above** the 9:00–10:00 H1 candle's high, a later 5-minute candle closes back **at or below** that high (a reversal candidate), and a subsequent 5-minute candle's low then trades **below** that reversal candle's low (breakdown confirmation), the strategy enters a **short** (sell) position, managed with the mirror-image exits (stop-loss above entry, take-profit near the H1 low, break-even when price moves far enough in favor).

**Why this priority**: The original backtest only captured long reversals off the H1 low, silently ignoring the equally common symmetric short setup off the H1 high — so it under-reported how the strategy would actually have traded. Catching both directions is core to the improved backtest's value.

**Independent Test**: Run the backtest against a known historical day for FRA40.I where price broke above the 9:00–10:00 H1 high and reversed. Verify the reported short trade (entry at the reversal candle's low or the confirming candle's open on a gap, exit, exit reason, and points) matches manual calculation using the mirrored rules.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a trading day where, after 10:00, FRA40.I trades above the H1 high, a later 5-minute candle closes back at/below that high, and a subsequent candle's low then trades below that reversal candle's low, **When** the backtest runs, **Then** the system reports a short trade entered at the reversal candle's low (or at the confirming candle's open price if it gapped below that low), recorded with direction "short".
2. **Given** an open short from Scenario 1, **When** price rises to a loss of 50 points from the entry (entry + 50) before any other exit, **Then** the trade closes at that stop-loss level (or the candle's open on a gap above it).
3. **Given** an open short from Scenario 1, **When** price falls to reach the H1 low plus 10 points before the stop-loss or end of day, **Then** the trade closes at that take-profit level (or the candle's open on a gap below it).
4. **Given** an open short from Scenario 1, **When** a 5-minute candle's low reaches 20 points or more below the entry price, **Then** the short's stop-loss moves down to the entry price (break-even) for the following candles, at most once, mirroring the long-side break-even rule.
5. **Given** a flat day evaluating both directions, **When** a valid long breakout and a valid short breakdown are both available, **Then** at most one position is open at any time — whichever confirms first holds the floor, and the other direction is only reconsidered once the position closes.

### User Story 1c - Cut a stalled position 30 minutes after entry (time-cut variant) (Priority: P2)

A trader wants a second version of the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest that behaves identically but abandons a position that fails to show momentum: if, 30 minutes after entry, the trade has never moved more than 5 points in its favor, it is closed at market rather than left to run to its stop or the end of day. The trader can run this "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)" backtest from the same menu and compare its results against the original.

**Why this priority**: The trader's hypothesis is that a setup which hasn't moved in its favor within half an hour is unlikely to work and only ties up the position; cutting it early may improve the strategy's net result. Testing that hypothesis needs the variant to exist as its own comparable backtest, but the original backtest (Stories 1/1b) already delivers value without it.

**Independent Test**: Run the time-cut backtest against a day where a trade enters and then drifts sideways (its high, for a long, never reaching entry + 5) for at least 30 minutes. Verify it reports a time-cut exit at the close of the first 5-minute candle at or after the 30-minute mark, and that the same day on the original backtest instead runs to its normal exit.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an open trade on the time-cut backtest that, 30 minutes after entry, has never moved more than 5 points in its favor (for a long, its high never reached entry + 5; for a short, its low never reached entry − 5), **When** the first 5-minute candle at or after the 30-minute mark is evaluated, **Then** the trade is closed at that candle's close, recorded with exit reason "time-cut".
2. **Given** an open trade on the time-cut backtest that did move more than 5 points in its favor at some point within the first 30 minutes, **When** the 30-minute mark passes, **Then** the trade is not cut and continues under the normal stop-loss / take-profit / break-even / end-of-day rules.
3. **Given** an open trade on the time-cut backtest whose best favorable move by the 30-minute mark was exactly 5 points, **When** that mark is evaluated, **Then** the trade is still cut ("never been higher than 5 points" is inclusive of exactly 5).
4. **Given** an open trade that reaches its stop-loss or take-profit before the 30-minute mark, **When** that exit is reached, **Then** it closes on that exit as usual and the time-cut never applies.
5. **Given** the same trading day, **When** it is run on the original "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest, **Then** no trade is ever closed for the time-cut reason — the original backtest is unaffected by this rule.

### User Story 1d - Trade only wide-range days with a structural H1 stop (Priority: P2)

A trader wants a third version of the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest to test two ideas together: only take days whose 9:00–10:00 range is genuinely wide (more than 40 points), and, instead of a fixed 50-point stop, let each trade run until the H1 structure breaks — a 5-minute candle closing back beyond the H1 reference level. The trader runs this "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (wide-range structural stop)" backtest from the same menu and compares it against the original and the time-cut variant.

**Why this priority**: The trader's hypothesis (from the regime analysis) is that B9H's edge, if any, is on wide-range mornings, and that a structure-based stop fits the setup better than an arbitrary fixed distance. Testing it needs the variant to exist as its own comparable backtest; the original backtests already deliver value without it.

**Independent Test**: Run this backtest across a range and verify (a) every day whose H1 range is 40 points or less is reported as "no trade" with zero trades, and days above 40 points trade normally; and (b) on a wide-range day where, after entry and before break-even arms, a 5-minute candle closes back below the H1 low, the trade exits at that candle's close with a stop-loss reason — where the original backtest would instead have run to its entry-minus-50 stop.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a day whose H1 (9:00–10:00) range is 40 points or less, **When** this backtest is run for that day, **Then** no candidate search is performed and the day is reported as "no trade" (zero trades), regardless of any breakout that occurs.
2. **Given** a day whose H1 range is strictly greater than 40 points, **When** this backtest is run, **Then** candidates and trades are evaluated exactly as in the base strategy (FR-006–FR-007), subject to the changed stop.
3. **Given** an open long whose break-even has not armed, **When** a 5-minute candle closes below the H1 low, **Then** the trade is closed at that candle's close, recorded with a stop-loss exit.
4. **Given** an open long that has moved at least +20 points favorable (break-even armed, stop at entry), **When** a later candle pulls back to the entry price, **Then** it closes at break-even per the base rule — the structural stop no longer applies once break-even is armed.
5. **Given** an open long whose break-even has not armed, **When** a 5-minute candle's high reaches the take-profit level (H1 high − 10), **Then** it closes on take-profit as in the base strategy (structural stop and take-profit coexist unchanged).
6. **Given** the mirror short, **When** a 5-minute candle closes above the H1 high before break-even arms, **Then** the short is closed at that candle's close with a stop-loss exit.
7. **Given** the same wide-range day, **When** it is run on the original "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest, **Then** the stop is the fixed entry-minus-50 level, not the structural close-beyond-H1 exit — confirming the variants differ only by the range filter and the stop.

### User Story 2 - Run the backtest over a UI-provided time range and see aggregate results (Priority: P2)

A trader wants to enter a start date and end date in the UI, run the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest across every trading day in that range, and see one summary result: number of days, number of trades, number of winning positions, number of losing positions, number of break-even positions, average win, average loss, and the final (net) result — to judge whether the strategy is worth trading live.

**Why this priority**: A single day's result has little statistical value; traders need to see the strategy's behavior across a meaningful sample of days before trusting it. This builds directly on User Story 1's per-day logic.

**Independent Test**: Provide a known multi-week start/end date range with a mix of trade and no-trade days, run the backtest, and verify the eight summary figures (number of days, number of trades, number of winning positions, number of losing positions, number of break-even positions, average win, average loss, final result) match a manual computation from the individual per-day results.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a start date and end date entered in the UI, **When** the backtest runs, **Then** the system displays a single summary containing: number of days, number of trades, number of winning positions, number of losing positions, number of break-even (BE) positions, average win (in points), average loss (in points), and the final result (net points) for the range.
2. **Given** a date range that includes non-trading days (weekends, holidays) or days with missing H1 data, **When** the backtest runs, **Then** those days are excluded from the "number of days" count and from every other summary figure, and the run completes without error.
3. **Given** a date range where no trade signal ever occurs, **When** the backtest runs, **Then** the system displays number of trades as 0, number of winning, losing, and break-even positions as 0, average win and average loss as not applicable, and a final result of 0 points.
4. **Given** a date range containing at least one trade that closed via the break-even mechanism, **When** the backtest runs, **Then** that trade counts toward "number of BE" and toward "number of trades," but not toward "number of winning positions" or "number of losing positions," and its 0-point result is excluded from the average win and average loss calculations.
5. **Given** an end date before the start date, or a start or end date in the future, entered in the UI, **When** the trader submits the request, **Then** the system rejects it with a validation error and does not run the backtest.
6. **Given** a completed range run, **When** the trader requests a CSV export, **Then** the system downloads a CSV file with one row per day in the range results (date, status, trade count, points), matching the displayed summary.

### User Story 3 - Inspect the reference levels and candles behind a day's result (Priority: P3)

A trader wants to see, for a given backtested day, the H1 opening-range high/low, the 5-minute candles used, and where the entry and exit fell, so they can visually verify the backtest matched the actual chart.

**Why this priority**: Builds trust in the hardcoded logic by letting a trader audit individual results, but the strategy is usable (Stories 1–2) without this inspection view.

**Independent Test**: Open the detail view for a day that produced a trade and confirm the displayed H1 high/low, the 5-minute candle sequence, and the marked entry/exit points are consistent with the values reported in the summary for that day.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a backtested day with a trade, **When** the trader opens that day's detail view, **Then** the system shows the 9:00–10:00 H1 high and low, the sequence of 5-minute candles from 10:00 onward, and the entry and exit points marked against that data.
2. **Given** a backtested day's detail view, **When** the trader requests a CSV export, **Then** the system downloads a CSV file containing the H1 high/low, the 5-minute candle sequence, and the day's trades.

### Edge Cases

- **No H1 reference candle**: If the 9:00–10:00 H1 candle cannot be retrieved for a day (holiday, missing data, market closed), that day is skipped and reported as "no data," not as "no trade."
- **Breakdown without confirmed reversal**: Price trades below the H1 low after 10:00 but no later 5-minute candle closes back above it before the session ends — no trade is taken for that day.
- **Reversal without breakout confirmation**: A 5-minute candle closes back above the H1 low (becoming a candidate, FR-006), but no later candle's high ever trades above that candidate's high before the session ends — no trade is taken for that day, even though a reversal did occur.
- **Candidate rolls forward instead of resetting**: While waiting for breakout confirmation, a candle that stays at or above the H1 low but fails to exceed the current candidate's high does not cancel the signal — it becomes the new candidate itself (FR-006b), so the breakout level to watch keeps moving forward with each such candle.
- **Candidate discarded by a fresh close below the H1 low**: While waiting for breakout confirmation, a candle that closes back below the H1 low discards the pending candidate outright (FR-006b) — because closing below the H1 low is itself the breach criterion, this same candle immediately counts as a new breakdown (FR-006), rather than requiring a separate candle to re-trigger one.
- **Repeat breakdown after a closed trade**: Once a trade has closed for the day (stop-loss, take-profit, or end of day), the strategy re-arms and MUST take a new trade if price breaks below the H1 low again, a later 5-minute candle closes back above it, and a subsequent candle confirms the breakout before the session ends — there is no cap on the number of trades in a day, other than requiring the previous trade to be closed first (never more than one open position at a time).
- **Stop-loss (or break-even) and take-profit breached in the same 5-minute candle**: Whichever stop level is currently active (the original stop-loss, or break-even once armed) is assumed to trigger first (conservative outcome) when a single candle's range would have touched both it and the take-profit level.
- **Break-even arms in the same candle it would otherwise have exited on take-profit or the original stop-loss**: The exit (stop-loss or take-profit) is resolved first, using the stop-loss level in effect at the start of that candle; the break-even arming (based on the candle's high reaching entry + 20 points) only takes effect starting with the next candle, and is moot if the trade has already exited.
- **Break-even arms and is then breached in the same candle**: Since the stop only moves to break-even starting the next candle (see above), a single candle's high reaching entry + 20 points cannot, by itself, also trigger a break-even exit in that same candle — at least one further candle is required to break back down through the entry price.
- **Gap through the exit level**: If the 5-minute candle that breaches the stop-loss, break-even, or take-profit level opens beyond that level (a gap), the exit is recorded at that candle's open price rather than the exact threshold, to reflect realistic fill slippage. The same convention applies to entry (FR-007): if the confirming breakout candle opens above the candidate's high, the entry is recorded at that open price instead of the exact high.
- **End-of-day with no clear last candle**: The end-of-day exit uses the close of the final 5-minute candle of FRA40.I's regular trading session for that day.
- **Break-even state does not carry across trades**: Each trade (including a same-day re-entry per FR-011) starts with its own fresh stop-loss at entry-minus-50; the break-even arming from a previous, already-closed trade has no effect on a later trade that day.
- **Invalid date range submitted**: If the end date is before the start date, or either date is in the future, the system MUST reject the request with a validation error before evaluating any day, rather than running and returning an empty or zero-trade result.
- **Confirmed breakout too far from the H1 low, or already at/past take-profit**: A breakout can be confirmed (FR-006b) at an entry price that ends up more than 20 points above the H1 low, or at/above the take-profit level itself (FR-006a) — for example a candidate whose high already sat far from the low, or a confirming candle that gaps up sharply. No trade is opened in this case; the strategy immediately resumes looking for a fresh breakdown below the H1 low (FR-006), the same as if no candidate had ever formed.
- **Short side is the mirror of every long edge case (2026-07-21)**: All the long-side edge cases above have a symmetric short counterpart around the H1 high — breach-without-reversal, reversal-without-breakdown-confirmation, candidate rolling forward, candidate discarded by a fresh close above the high, repeat short after a closed trade, same-candle stop-vs-take-profit, break-even arm/breach timing, gap through the exit, and confirmed-breakdown-too-far-below-the-high (or already at/below the short take-profit). Read "above the high / below the low" and "rises / falls" swapped throughout.
- **One position at a time across both directions (2026-07-21)**: While any position is open, neither a long nor a short signal is acted on; both searches only resume once flat. Whichever direction confirms a valid breakout first opens the position. A same-candle double confirmation resolves to the long (FR-023).
- **Opposite breach coincides with the open position's take-profit (2026-07-21)**: Because the short reference (H1 high) sits above the long take-profit (H1 high − 10), and the long reference (H1 low) sits below the short take-profit (H1 low + 10), the price move that would arm the opposite direction is the same move that reaches the open position's take-profit. In practice this means the two directions are naturally mutually exclusive: the candle that breaks the opposite extreme closes the current position on take-profit, and that closing candle is not re-evaluated for a new signal — so a genuinely concurrent long-and-short can never arise, and the FR-023 tiebreak is a defensive rule rather than a routinely-hit path.
- **Time-cut applies only to the variant (2026-07-22)**: The time-cut rule (FR-029) belongs to the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)" backtest alone; the original "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" never cuts on time. Both backtests trade both directions and share the four tunable thresholds (FR-025); they differ only by this one added exit.
- **Favorable move measured from entry (2026-07-22)**: The 5-point favorable move is the trade's max favorable excursion since entry — for a long, the highest its candle high has reached above the entry price; for a short, the lowest its candle low has reached below the entry price. The entry candle itself is not re-examined (entry is intrabar); measurement begins with the first candle after entry, consistent with break-even arming (FR-008a).
- **30-minute mark falls on a specific candle (2026-07-22)**: "30 minutes after entry" is the first 5-minute candle whose time is at or after entry time + 30 minutes. With entry recorded at the confirming candle's time (FR-007/FR-021) on FRA40.I's 5-minute grid, this is the 6th candle after entry. If the session ends before that mark (entry within 30 minutes of the close), the trade closes on the normal end-of-day exit and the time-cut never fires.
- **Time-cut is not a price-level exit (2026-07-22)**: Unlike stop-loss / take-profit / break-even, the time-cut fills at the candle's close, not at a threshold price, so the gap-fill convention (FR-010) does not apply to it.
- **Exit ordering with the time-cut (2026-07-22)**: On the candle at or after the 30-minute mark, the stop-loss/break-even and take-profit checks (FR-008/FR-009/FR-022) are resolved first; only if neither closed the trade is the time-cut applied, ahead of the end-of-day fallback.
- **Cache is per backtest definition, not shared across them (2026-07-24)**: "CAC40 Bougie de 9h", "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)", and "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (wide-range structural stop)" each cache the same FRA40.I candles for a shared day under separate keys (their own definition codes) — a day cached by one is not automatically a cache hit for another, even though the underlying candles are identical. This is a deliberate simplicity tradeoff (FR-036), not a defect.
- **A strategy or threshold change does not require clearing the cache (2026-07-24)**: Because only raw candles are cached (FR-039), changing the tunable thresholds (FR-025) between two runs of the same day, or changing the strategy logic itself in a later code change, produces different `Trade`/`DayResult` output on the next run without any cache invalidation step — the cache is never a source of stale computed results, only of stale (never-expiring, by design) raw candle inputs.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: System MUST provide a "Backtest" menu, reachable from primary navigation, listing the available hardcoded backtests by name.
- **FR-002**: System MUST provide one hardcoded backtest, "CAC40 Bougie de 9h," selectable from the Backtest menu, implementing the rules in FR-003 through FR-011 (including FR-006a, FR-006b and FR-008a). This backtest's *rules* (reference-range derivation, breakout/reversal detection, exit ordering, one-position-at-a-time) are fixed in code; the four numeric thresholds those rules use are tunable per run (FR-025–FR-027), defaulting to the values stated inline throughout this spec. Making those magnitudes tunable does not make this a generic engine: the menu still offers a fixed set of hardcoded strategies, not a tool for defining new ones.
- **FR-003**: For a given trading day, system MUST derive the reference range from the FRA40.I 1-hour (H1) candle covering 9:00–10:00 Paris exchange local time (Europe/Paris, DST-aware), using its high as the upper reference level and its low as the lower reference level. These levels stay fixed for the rest of the day, across all trades taken that day.
- **FR-004**: If the 9:00–10:00 (Paris local time) H1 candle is not available for a given day, system MUST report that day as having no data and MUST continue processing any other requested days without failing the run.
- **FR-005**: For the period from 10:00 Paris local time to the end of the trading session, system MUST evaluate FRA40.I 5-minute candles in chronological order.
- **FR-006**: System MUST recognize a breakdown-and-reversal candidate each time, after 10:00, a 5-minute candle closes below the H1 low (the breach, measured on the close — not an intrabar wick) and a later 5-minute candle closes at or above the H1 low, provided no trade is currently open and no candidate is already pending. That reversal candle becomes the pending candidate; closing back above the H1 low does not by itself produce a trade (see FR-006b for the breakout confirmation that does). This allows multiple candidates — and multiple trades — on the same day, as long as they occur sequentially rather than concurrently.
- **FR-006b**: A pending candidate (FR-006) only produces a trade once a later 5-minute candle's high trades above the candidate's high ("breakout confirmation"). Until that happens, each subsequent 5-minute candle is resolved as follows, in order:
  - If its high trades above the candidate's high, the breakout is confirmed (see FR-007 for the resulting entry).
  - Otherwise, if it closes back below the H1 low, the candidate is discarded and the search returns to looking for a fresh breakdown-and-reversal pair (FR-006) — this candle's close below the H1 low is itself the breach criterion, so it doubles as that fresh breakdown.
  - Otherwise (it stays at or above the H1 low without breaking the candidate's high), it replaces the candidate: the breakout level to watch rolls forward to this candle's high.
- **FR-006a**: A confirmed breakout (FR-006b) only produces a trade if the resulting entry price (FR-007) is both (a) no more than 20 points above the H1 low, and (b) strictly below the take-profit level (H1 high minus 10 points, FR-008). A breakout that satisfies FR-006b but fails either bound is not a valid entry: no trade is opened, and the strategy immediately resumes searching for a fresh breakdown-and-reversal pair (FR-006), rather than waiting out the rest of the day. This prevents entries that would leave little or no room to reach take-profit, or that would already be at or past it (added after PR review).
- **FR-007**: When a breakout is confirmed and valid (FR-006b, FR-006a), system MUST record a long (buy) entry at the candidate reversal candle's high — or, if the confirming 5-minute candle's open is above that high (a gap), at that candle's open price instead, per the same gap-fill convention used for exits (FR-010). The entry time is the confirming candle's time, not the candidate reversal candle's.
- **FR-008**: System MUST evaluate, for each 5-minute candle after entry in chronological order, whether any of the following exit conditions is met, and MUST close the trade at the first one reached:
  - **Stop-loss**: price has fallen to or below the trade's currently active stop-loss level (starting at 50 points below the entry price; see FR-008a for how this level can move).
  - **Take-profit**: price has risen to reach the H1 high minus 10 points.
  - **End of day**: the trading session for that day ends with neither of the above reached.
- **FR-008a**: While a trade is open and its stop-loss has not yet been moved, system MUST move that trade's stop-loss up to the entry price ("break-even") the moment a 5-minute candle's high reaches the entry price plus 20 points or more. This move happens at most once per trade, only ever moves the stop-loss upward (never back down toward entry-minus-50), and takes effect starting with the next 5-minute candle evaluated (see the "break-even arms in the same candle" edge case for same-candle handling). A trade subsequently closed at this moved level is recorded with exit reason "break-even," not "stop-loss," and a points result of 0 in the normal case — see FR-010 for the gap-through exception, where the recorded points can be a small non-zero value.
- **FR-009**: When both the currently active stop-loss level (original or break-even) and the take-profit level would be reached within the same 5-minute candle, system MUST resolve the exit using the stop-loss (or break-even) level.
- **FR-010**: When the 5-minute candle that triggers a stop-loss, break-even, or take-profit exit opens beyond that level (a gap), system MUST record the exit at that candle's open price; otherwise the exit MUST be recorded at the exact stop-loss, break-even, or take-profit price level. This applies uniformly to all three exit types, including break-even: a break-even exit is filled like any other stop, so a gap through the entry price on an armed trade records that trade's actual (small, non-zero) points result rather than forcing it to exactly 0 — the trade is still labeled and counted as "break-even" (FR-008a, FR-013), because that label reflects which mechanism closed the trade, not its resulting point value.
- **FR-011**: System MUST allow multiple sequential trades per day for this backtest: once a trade has closed (stop-loss, break-even, take-profit, or end of day) and there is remaining time before end of day, system MUST resume evaluating 5-minute candles for a new trade signal per FR-006. At most one trade may be open at any given time, and each new trade starts with its own fresh (unmoved) stop-loss.
- **FR-012**: System MUST let a trader provide a start date and end date via the UI and run the backtest across every trading day in that range (a single-day run is the special case where start date equals end date), with no application-imposed maximum range; the system MUST produce a per-day result (no data / no trade / one or more trades, each in chronological order with entry, exit, exit reason — stop-loss, break-even, take-profit, or end of day — and points gained or lost) for each day in the range. The detail view (User Story 3, FR-015) for any individual day is available on demand and is computed independently when requested — it is not required to reuse or cache the per-day result produced during the range run itself (consistent with the "ephemeral, not persisted" decision in Assumptions). Any day outside what the underlying candle history can supply is reported as "no data" (FR-004), not rejected upfront.
- **FR-013**: For a range run, system MUST display a single aggregate summary with exactly these figures:
  - **Number of days**: count of trading days in the range that had usable H1 reference data (days reported as "no data" are excluded).
  - **Number of trades**: total count of trades taken across the range (equal to the sum of winning, losing, and break-even positions).
  - **Number of winning positions**: count of trades, excluding break-even trades (see below), that closed with a positive points result.
  - **Number of losing positions**: count of trades, excluding break-even trades (see below), that closed with a zero or negative points result.
  - **Number of BE**: count of trades that closed via the break-even mechanism (FR-008a), classified by that mechanism regardless of their exact points result — normally exactly 0, but see FR-010's gap-through exception. A trade is never double-counted: break-even classification takes priority over the winning/losing points-sign check.
  - **Average win**: mean points gained across winning positions (not applicable when there are no winning positions).
  - **Average loss**: mean points lost across losing positions, expressed as a positive magnitude (not applicable when there are no losing positions). Break-even positions are always excluded from both this average and "average win," regardless of their exact points result.
  - **Final result**: net points gained or lost across every trade in the range (sum of all trades' points results).
- **FR-014**: Trade results MUST be expressed in index points (entry/exit price and points gained or lost), consistent with how the strategy's thresholds (50 points, 10 points) are defined.
- **FR-015**: System MUST let a trader open a detail view for any backtested day that produced at least one trade, showing the H1 high/low reference levels, the 5-minute candles used from 10:00 onward, and the entry/exit points for every trade taken that day.
- **FR-016**: System MUST validate the trader-provided start and end date before running the backtest, and MUST reject the request with a clear error, without evaluating any day, when the end date is before the start date or when either date is in the future.
- **FR-017**: System MUST let a trader export a range run's day-by-day summary as a downloadable CSV file, with one row per day in the results (date, status, trade count, points) — the same set of days already returned by the range run (FR-012).
- **FR-018**: System MUST let a trader export a single day's detail (FR-015) as a downloadable CSV file, containing the H1 high/low reference levels, the sequence of 5-minute candles (date, open, high, low, close), and the day's trades (entry time/price, exit time/price, exit reason, points).

#### Short positions (added 2026-07-21)

These requirements extend the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest so it trades both directions. The long-side rules (FR-003 through FR-011) are unchanged; the following add the mirror-image short side and the rule that governs running both directions together. All short-side thresholds are the mirror of the long side (same 50 / 10 / 20-point magnitudes), reflected around the H1 high instead of the H1 low.

- **FR-019**: The "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest MUST evaluate both the long side (FR-003–FR-011) and the short side (FR-020–FR-022) on every trading day. There is no direction toggle and no long-only mode; a day's result is the combined, chronological list of whatever long and short trades the rules produced.
- **FR-020**: System MUST recognize a short breakout-and-reversal candidate each time, after 10:00, a 5-minute candle closes **above** the H1 high (the breach, measured on the close — not an intrabar wick) and a later 5-minute candle closes **at or below** the H1 high, provided no position is open and no short candidate is already pending. That reversal candle becomes the pending short candidate. The candidate only produces a trade once a later 5-minute candle's **low** trades **below** the candidate's low ("breakdown confirmation"). Until then, each subsequent candle is resolved, in order: (a) if its low trades below the candidate's low, the breakout is confirmed; (b) otherwise if it closes back **above** the H1 high, the candidate is discarded and this candle doubles as a fresh breach above the high; (c) otherwise it replaces the candidate (the level to watch rolls forward to this candle's low). This mirrors FR-006/FR-006b reflected around the H1 high.
- **FR-020a**: A confirmed short breakout only produces a trade if the resulting entry price is both (a) no more than 20 points **below** the H1 high, and (b) strictly **above** the short take-profit level (H1 low plus 10 points). Otherwise no trade is opened and the search resumes for a fresh short breach above the high — the mirror of FR-006a.
- **FR-021**: When a short breakout is confirmed and valid, system MUST record a **short (sell)** entry at the candidate reversal candle's low — or, if the confirming candle's open is **below** that low (a gap), at that open price instead. The entry time is the confirming candle's time. Mirror of FR-007.
- **FR-022**: For an open short, system MUST evaluate each subsequent 5-minute candle for exits, closing at the first reached: **stop-loss** when price rises to or above the currently active stop level (starting at 50 points **above** the entry price); **take-profit** when price falls to reach the H1 low plus 10 points; **end of day** otherwise. While the short's stop has not moved, it MUST move down to the entry price ("break-even") the moment a candle's **low** reaches the entry price minus 20 points or more (at most once, taking effect from the next candle). The stop-vs-take-profit same-candle priority (FR-009), the gap-fill convention (FR-010), and the exit-reason labels (stop-loss / break-even / take-profit / end of day) all apply identically, mirrored around the H1 high.
- **FR-023**: System MUST run both directions with **at most one open position at any time**. While a position (long or short) is open, no new signal of either direction is acted on; the search for both directions resumes only once the position has closed. Whichever direction confirms a valid breakout first opens the position. On the rare candle that would confirm both a long and a short entry simultaneously, the long entry takes precedence (deterministic tiebreak). Each new position starts with its own fresh (unmoved) stop, and there is no cap on the number of trades per day beyond the one-position-at-a-time constraint (extends FR-011 across both directions).
- **FR-024**: System MUST record each trade's direction (long or short) and expose it in both the per-day detail (FR-015) and the single-day/detail responses, so a trader can tell which side each trade was on. The aggregate summary figures (FR-013) are computed on the trade's signed points result regardless of direction: a short whose exit is below its entry is a winning position, and so on. The CSV exports (FR-017/FR-018) include the trade direction column as well.

#### Parametrized thresholds (added 2026-07-21)

These requirements make the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest's four numeric thresholds tunable per run. They do not change the strategy logic — every rule in FR-003–FR-024 still holds, with the fixed magnitudes those requirements state (50, 10, 20 points) reinterpreted as the *default* values of the corresponding parameters. Both directions use the same parameter values (the short side mirrors the long side, per the "Short positions" note).

- **FR-025**: The backtest MUST accept, for each run (single day or range, and the matching CSV exports), an optional set of four strategy thresholds:
  - **Stop-loss distance** — points from entry to the initial stop (FR-008 / FR-022). Default **50**.
  - **Take-profit offset** — points inside the opposite H1 level at which take-profit sits: H1 high − offset for a long, H1 low + offset for a short (FR-008 / FR-022). Default **10**.
  - **Break-even trigger** — favorable move (from entry) that arms the break-even stop (FR-008a / FR-022). Default **20**.
  - **Max entry distance** — maximum distance from the H1 reference level for a confirmed breakout to be a valid entry (FR-006a / FR-020a). Default **20**.
- **FR-026**: Any threshold a run does not supply MUST fall back to its default (FR-025), so a run that supplies none behaves exactly as the pre-parametrization backtest. The parameters are per-run inputs only — not persisted, not global configuration — consistent with the "ephemeral, not persisted" decision for Backtest Runs (Assumptions).
- **FR-027**: The system MUST validate each supplied threshold as strictly greater than 0 and MUST reject the request with a validation error, without evaluating any day, when a threshold is non-positive — the same up-front, no-partial-work stance as the date-range validation (FR-016).

#### Time-cut variant (added 2026-07-22)

These requirements add a second hardcoded backtest, "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)", that reuses the entire "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" rule set (FR-003–FR-027) and adds one exit. The original backtest is unchanged and gains no new behavior.

- **FR-028**: System MUST provide a second hardcoded backtest, "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)", selectable from the Backtest menu (FR-001) alongside "CAC40 Bougie de 9h". It applies every rule of "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" — reference range, both-direction breakout/reversal detection, entry validity, exit ordering, one-position-at-a-time, and the four tunable thresholds (FR-025) — and adds only the time-cut exit (FR-029). This does not turn the menu into a generic engine (FR-002): it is a second fixed strategy, not a tool for authoring strategies.
- **FR-029**: For an open position on the time-cut backtest, once 30 minutes have elapsed since entry (FR-007/FR-021), if the position has never moved more than 5 points in its favor since entry — its max favorable excursion (for a long, highest candle high minus entry; for a short, entry minus lowest candle low) is 5 points or less — system MUST close it at market on the first 5-minute candle whose time is at or after entry time + 30 minutes, at that candle's close, recorded with a new exit reason "time-cut". "Never been higher than 5 points" is inclusive: a max favorable excursion of exactly 5 points is cut.
- **FR-030**: The time-cut exit (FR-029) MUST be evaluated after the stop-loss/break-even and take-profit exits (FR-008/FR-009/FR-022) on the same candle — those take priority if reached — and before the end-of-day fallback (FR-008). A position that reaches its stop or target before the 30-minute mark closes on that exit and is never time-cut; a position entered within 30 minutes of the session close is never time-cut and closes end-of-day.
- **FR-031**: The 30-minute duration and 5-point favorable-move threshold of the time-cut are fixed properties of this variant, not added to the tunable Backtest Parameters (FR-025); the four existing thresholds still apply identically to both backtests. The "time-cut" exit reason MUST be recorded and exposed in the per-day detail (FR-015), the day/summary responses, and the CSV exports (FR-017/FR-018) the same way as the other exit reasons (FR-024). In the aggregate summary (FR-013) a time-cut trade is classified by the sign of its points result — a winning or losing position like any non-break-even exit — never as a break-even.

These requirements add a third hardcoded backtest, "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (wide-range structural stop)", that reuses the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" rule set (FR-003–FR-027) and changes exactly two things: an H1-range entry filter (FR-033) and the stop-loss mechanism (FR-034). The first two backtests are unchanged.

- **FR-032**: System MUST provide a third hardcoded backtest, "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (wide-range structural stop)", selectable from the Backtest menu (FR-001) alongside the other two. It applies every rule of "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" — reference range, both-direction breakout/reversal detection, entry validity (FR-006a), break-even (FR-008a), take-profit, exit ordering, one-position-at-a-time — except as modified by FR-033 (range filter) and FR-034 (structural stop). This does not turn the menu into a generic engine (FR-002): it is a third fixed strategy.
- **FR-033**: For this variant, a trading day is eligible for trades only when its H1 reference range (H1 high − H1 low, FR-003) is **strictly greater than 40 points**. On a day whose H1 range is 40 points or less, system MUST take no trades — performing no candidate search — and report the day as "no trade" (FR-012), not "no data" (the H1 candle is present). The 40-point threshold is a fixed property of this variant, not added to the tunable Backtest Parameters (FR-025).
- **FR-034**: For this variant, the initial stop-loss (FR-008's "50 points below the entry price") is replaced by a **structural stop**: while a trade is open and its break-even has not yet armed (FR-008a), system MUST close it the moment a 5-minute candle **closes** beyond the H1 reference level on the losing side — for a long, a candle whose close is below the H1 low; for a short, a candle whose close is above the H1 high — at that candle's close, recorded with the "stop-loss" exit reason. Because this is a market (close) exit, not a price-level exit, the FR-010 gap-fill convention does not apply. The tunable stop-loss distance (FR-025, default 50) is unused by this variant.
- **FR-035**: Break-even (FR-008a) and take-profit (FR-008) apply to this variant unchanged. Once break-even arms (+20 points favorable) the stop moves to the entry price and that break-even stop becomes the active stop, **superseding** the structural stop (FR-034) — consistent with break-even only ever moving the stop upward; the structural stop therefore applies only while break-even is unarmed. Exit ordering within one 5-minute candle: while unarmed, the structural stop is a **close** event (the candle's last tick) whereas take-profit is reached intrabar (earlier), so a candle that both trades to take-profit and closes beyond the H1 level closes on **take-profit**; once break-even is armed, the stop is a price level and the base FR-009 ordering applies (break-even before take-profit). The three still-applicable thresholds (take-profit offset, break-even trigger, max entry distance — FR-025) are in effect; exit reasons and summary classification (FR-013/FR-024) are unchanged from the base.

#### Backtest candle caching (added 2026-07-24)

These requirements reduce repeated Saxo calls across runs by caching the *raw candle data* a day's evaluation depends on — never the computed strategy result. They apply identically to all three hardcoded backtests (FR-002, FR-028, FR-032).

- **FR-036**: Before evaluating a trading day, system MUST look up a cached entry keyed by (backtest definition code, trading date). If found, system MUST use the cached data — the 9:00–10:00 H1 reference candle and, when available, the 5-minute candles from 10:00 to end of day — instead of fetching them from Saxo again.
- **FR-037**: If no cached entry exists for a (backtest definition code, trading date) pair, system MUST fetch the day's H1 reference candle and, when it is available, its 5-minute candles from Saxo as today, then MUST store them under that key before (or as part of) evaluating the day, so later requests for the same pair are served from the cache.
- **FR-038**: A day for which the H1 reference candle is unavailable (FR-004, "no data") MUST also be cached as such under the (backtest definition code, trading date) key, so a later request for the same pair does not re-query Saxo only to find the same day still has no data.
- **FR-039**: The strategy evaluation (reference-range derivation, breakout/reversal detection, exits, and the tunable thresholds of FR-025) MUST always be executed fresh against the day's candles — cached or freshly fetched — on every request. No computed trade, exit reason, or summary figure is ever itself cached; only the underlying raw candle data is (Clarifications, Session 2026-07-24).
- **FR-040**: Cached candle entries MUST NOT expire (no TTL). Correcting a cached entry that is later found to hold incorrect data is a manual data-maintenance action outside this feature's scope, not a capability this feature provides.

### Key Entities

- **Backtest Definition**: A hardcoded strategy available in the Backtest menu (name, instrument, fixed rule set, and any variant-specific fixed properties — e.g. the time-cut's 30-minute / 5-point settings (FR-029), or the wide-range variant's 40-point range filter and structural stop (FR-033/FR-034)). "CAC40 Bougie de 9h", its "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)" variant, and its "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (wide-range structural stop)" variant are the three instances; the data model must not assume it is the only one.
- **Backtest Parameters**: The four tunable thresholds a run may supply (stop-loss distance, take-profit offset, break-even trigger, max entry distance — FR-025). A per-run input with defaults reproducing the original hardcoded values; not persisted and not tied to a Backtest Definition (the same definition can be run with different parameters).
- **Backtest Run**: The result of executing a Backtest Definition over a UI-provided date range (a single day is the range's degenerate case) — a list of per-day outcomes plus the aggregate summary: number of days, number of trades, number of winning positions, number of losing positions, number of BE, average win, average loss, and final result. Computed on demand for the requesting trader and returned synchronously; not persisted (for now — see Assumptions).
- **Day Result**: The outcome for a single trading day within a Backtest Run — either "no data," "no trade," or a chronological list of one or more trades, each with direction (long or short), entry price/time, exit price/time, exit reason (stop-loss, break-even, take-profit, end of day, or — on the time-cut variant — time-cut), and points gained or lost. Trades of both directions may appear in the same day's list, ordered by time; at most one is open at any moment (FR-023).
- **Backtest Candle Cache** (added 2026-07-24): A persisted entry, one per (backtest definition code, trading date) pair, holding the raw Saxo candle data for that day — the 9:00–10:00 H1 reference candle (or an explicit "no data" marker) and, when available, the 5-minute candles from 10:00 to end of day. Populated the first time a day is evaluated for a given definition and read on every later request for that same pair; never expires. This is the only thing persisted by this feature — the computed Backtest Run/Day Result output is still produced fresh on every request (FR-039) and is not itself cached.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: A trader can select the Backtest menu, run the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h" backtest for a single past day, and see a correct result (no data / no trade / one or more trades, each with entry, exit, and points) within a few seconds of requesting it.
- **SC-002**: On a hand-verified set of at least 6 historical FRA40.I trading days covering all outcome types (no data, no trade, stop-loss exit, break-even exit, take-profit exit, end-of-day exit, and at least one day with more than one trade), the backtest's reported entry price, exit price, exit reason, and points result match manual calculation exactly for every trade on every day.
- **SC-003**: A trader can enter a start and end date in the UI, run the backtest over a multi-week range, and receive a summary (number of days, number of trades, number of winning positions, number of losing positions, number of BE, average win, average loss, final result) that matches a manual computation from the individual day results.
- **SC-004**: A trader can open the detail view for any day with a trade and visually confirm the H1 reference levels and the entry/exit points against the underlying 5-minute candles.
- **SC-005**: Days with missing or unavailable H1 reference data never interrupt a multi-day backtest run — the run always completes and reports results for every day that does have data.
- **SC-006**: A trader can run the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)" backtest over the same range as the original and see, for at least one hand-verified day where a trade stalled at 5 points or less favorable for 30 minutes, a time-cut exit at the expected candle close, while the original backtest closes that same trade on its normal exit — confirming the two backtests differ only by the time-cut rule.
- **SC-007**: A trader can run the "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (wide-range structural stop)" backtest over the same range as the original and see (a) every day whose H1 range is 40 points or less reported as "no trade" with zero trades, and (b) for at least one hand-verified wide-range day, a stop-loss exit at the close of the 5-minute candle that first closes back beyond the H1 level before break-even arms — where the original backtest instead runs to its entry-minus-50 stop — confirming the variant differs only by the range filter and the structural stop.
- **SC-008**: Re-running the same backtest definition over a date range that includes a day already evaluated in a previous run produces byte-for-byte identical per-day results for that day, without a new Saxo call being made for it (observable via reduced Saxo call volume / logs), confirming the candle cache (FR-036–FR-040) is transparent to the trader-facing result.

## Assumptions

- "Points" refers to raw index price difference on FRA40.I (e.g., entry 8000.0 to exit 7950.0 is a 50-point loss), not currency P&L from a specific position size; no position sizing or account currency conversion is in scope.
- The FRA40.I H1 and 5-minute candle history needed for past (fully closed) trading days is obtainable through the existing Saxo historical-candle capability; no new market-data source is required. The UI does not enforce its own maximum date-range length — a trader may request any range, and any day the underlying candle history cannot supply is simply reported as "no data" (FR-004) rather than rejected upfront.
- The backtest operates only on already-closed historical days; it does not run live or simulate intraday in real time.
- "End of day" means the close of FRA40.I's regular trading session for that day, using the last available 5-minute candle.
- All clock times in this spec (9:00, 10:00, end of day) are Paris exchange local time (Europe/Paris), which observes daylight saving time; on the rare day where DST transition affects candle availability for the reference window, the existing "no data" handling (FR-004) applies.
- Three hardcoded backtests are in scope for this feature — "CAC40 Bougie de 9h", its "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (time cut)" variant (added 2026-07-22), and its "CAC40 Bougie de 9h (wide-range structural stop)" variant (added 2026-07-24); the Backtest menu's list structure supports adding further hardcoded backtests later, but no generic backtest-authoring capability is being built.
- The wide-range structural-stop variant (FR-032–FR-035) reuses the base strategy and changes exactly two things: it trades only days whose H1 range exceeds 40 points (FR-033), and it replaces the fixed 50-point stop with a structural stop that fires when a 5-minute candle closes back beyond the H1 level while break-even is unarmed (FR-034), filling at that candle's close. Break-even and take-profit are unchanged; break-even supersedes the structural stop once armed. The 40-point threshold is a fixed property of the variant, not a run parameter, and the stop-loss-distance parameter (FR-025) is unused by it.
- The time-cut variant (FR-028–FR-031) reuses the base strategy verbatim and differs only by the added exit; its 30-minute / 5-point settings are hardcoded properties of that backtest, not run parameters (FR-025). The favorable move is measured as max favorable excursion from entry, starting with the first candle after entry (the entry candle is intrabar and not re-examined, mirroring break-even arming). A time-cut trade fills at the candle close (not a threshold price, so the FR-010 gap-fill does not apply) and is classified in the summary (FR-013) by the sign of its points result — normally a small loss or small gain — never as a break-even.
- The four tunable thresholds (FR-025) are the only parametrized inputs; the instrument, the 9:00–10:00 Paris reference window, and the 5-minute/H1 candle horizons remain fixed in code. Every numeric magnitude stated inline in FR-003–FR-024 (50, 10, 20 points) is the *default* of the corresponding parameter — a run may override it, and all downstream rules (entry validity, exit ordering, break-even arming, the short-side mirror) use the effective per-run value. Making the magnitudes tunable is not a generic engine; the strategy's logic is unchanged (Clarifications, Session 2026-07-21 parametrization).
- There is no upper limit on how many trades can occur in a single day beyond the natural constraint that a new trade can only start once the previous one has closed (never more than one open position at a time).
- No order placement, paper-trading, or live-execution capability is implied — this is a historical, read-only analysis feature.
- Backtest Run results are not persisted for this feature; each run is computed on demand and returned to the requesting trader synchronously. Saving/revisiting past runs is out of scope but not precluded for a future iteration. This is unchanged by the candle caching added 2026-07-24 (FR-036–FR-040): what is now persisted is only the raw Saxo candle data a day's evaluation depends on, keyed per (backtest definition, date); the computed `Trade`/`DayResult`/summary output is still recomputed on every request from that (cached or freshly fetched) candle data, and is never itself stored.
- A trade closed via the break-even mechanism (FR-008a) is counted only in "number of BE" — never in winning or losing positions, and excluded from the average win/loss calculations — regardless of its exact points result. Normally this is exactly 0 points, but FR-010's gap-fill rule applies uniformly to all exit types: if the exiting candle opens beyond the (now break-even) stop level, the trade's actual points result can be a small non-zero value. The "BE" label reflects the exit mechanism (the stop had been moved to break-even before it was hit), not a guarantee that the outcome was exactly flat — a deliberate resolution of what would otherwise be a contradiction between FR-008a's normal-case description and FR-010's gap-fill rule. In the rare case a trade reaches exactly 0 points through a *different* exit (e.g., an end-of-day close that happens to land on the entry price without break-even ever arming), it is classified as a losing position, since only break-even-mechanism exits are labeled "BE".
- "Average loss" is reported as a positive magnitude (e.g., "42 pts") rather than a negative number, so the summary reads naturally; "final result" is the only figure in the summary that can be negative, since it is a net sum.
- "Number of days" in the range summary counts only trading days where the H1 reference candle was available (i.e., days actually evaluated); days reported as "no data" are not counted, consistent with how they are excluded from every other aggregate figure.
- The break-even arm threshold (entry + 20 points) and the resulting stop level (entry price) are evaluated using candle high/low, not tick-level data; when a candle's high would both arm the break-even stop and (in the same candle) its low would breach it, the arming is treated as taking effect only from the next candle onward, so such a same-candle round-trip cannot itself produce a break-even exit — this favors a conservative, implementable reading over assuming a specific intra-candle price path.
